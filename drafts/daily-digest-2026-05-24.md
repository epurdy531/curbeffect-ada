**Both federal digital-accessibility deadlines for the public sector have now been pushed back roughly a year, and both extensions are open for public comment — but neither one narrows the legal exposure facing covered organizations. The DOJ moved its ADA Title II web and mobile deadlines for state and local governments to April 2027 (50,000+) and April 2028 (smaller entities and special districts), and HHS followed with parallel Section 504 dates of May 2027 and May 2028 for federally funded healthcare and education recipients; written comments on the DOJ rule are due June 22, with HHS running its own comment window. Disability-rights organizations — from the American Association of People with Disabilities to the American Council of the Blind — are mobilizing to oppose the delays as formal comments, a clear signal that scrutiny of public entities is intensifying, not fading. The point worth repeating to every covered organization is that an extended compliance date moves the clock, not the underlying duty: Title II's nondiscrimination obligations and Section 504's protections, on the books since 1973, remain fully enforceable, and private plaintiffs can sue today — federal website-accessibility filings rose in 2025, with governments, schools, universities, and healthcare providers a growing share of the targets. For any public entity or HHS-funded organization, the smartest use of the extra time is to keep remediating now rather than treat the new date as a reprieve.**
