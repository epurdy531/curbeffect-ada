Document Accessibility Tip of the Day

📃Topic: Nesting Word heading levels in the right order📄

Applying Word's Heading styles is only half the job — the *level* you choose for each one is what builds the document's outline. A screen reader user navigates by jumping heading to heading and relies on those levels to understand how sections nest inside one another. When the levels are out of order, the exported PDF inherits a broken structure that no amount of visual polish will fix.

Treat your headings like an outline: Heading 1 for the document or main section title, Heading 2 for the sections under it, Heading 3 for subsections, and so on. Don't skip levels — never jump from a Heading 2 straight to a Heading 4 just because you like how the smaller style looks. If a built-in level is the wrong size or color for your design, right-click the style and choose Modify to change its appearance rather than picking a different level to get the look you want. The level carries the meaning; the formatting is just paint.

Before you export, open View and turn on the Navigation Pane. It lists every heading in its nesting order, so a subsection that is sitting at the wrong level, or a "heading" that was really just bold body text and never got a style, jumps right out at you. Fix the order there, and Word's Save As PDF or Export will carry that same clean hierarchy into the PDF's tag tree and bookmarks — giving assistive technology a reliable map of the document instead of a flat pile of text.

🔍 Reference: for the exact normative wording, see PDF/UA clause 7.4 in your ISO 14289-1 copy.
