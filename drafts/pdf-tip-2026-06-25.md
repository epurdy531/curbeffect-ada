Document Accessibility Tip of the Day

📃Topic: Alt text in Word — decide decorative vs. meaningful, and don't trust auto-generated text📄

Adding alternative text in Word isn't just a matter of filling every box with something. The first decision for each image, chart, or shape is whether it actually conveys information, and Word gives you a tool for both answers.

Right-click any picture or object and choose "View Alt Text" (or open the Alt Text pane from the Picture Format tab). If the graphic is purely visual — a divider line, a background flourish, a repeated logo decoration — tick the "Mark as decorative" checkbox instead of writing a description. That checkbox tells the PDF export to tag the object as an artifact, so screen readers skip past it cleanly rather than announcing an empty or meaningless image. Skipping this step is exactly what leaves you hand-artifacting decorative images later in Acrobat.

For graphics that do carry meaning, write the description yourself and delete anything Word auto-suggested. Word will offer machine-generated alt text marked "(generated)" — it is often vague or wrong, and it carries straight into the exported PDF as the figure's /Alt entry. Replace it with a concise sentence stating the point of the image. This matters most for charts and SmartArt: a column chart needs the trend or the key numbers in words ("Permit applications rose from 120 in 2023 to 340 in 2025"), not "chart." SmartArt and grouped shapes should get one description on the whole group conveying the relationship it shows, rather than letting each piece announce itself separately.

Getting the decorative-versus-meaningful call right in Word means the exported PDF arrives with figures already tagged correctly and decorative items already artifacted — two of the most common remediation findings, handled before the file ever reaches a PDF tool.

🔍 Reference: for the exact normative wording, see PDF/UA clause 7.3 in your ISO 14289-1 copy.
