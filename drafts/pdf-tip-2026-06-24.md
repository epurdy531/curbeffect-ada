Document Accessibility Tip of the Day

📃Topic: Bookmarks and the document outline📄

In a long PDF, bookmarks are the navigation panel a reader opens on the left side of the viewer to jump straight to a section without scrolling page by page. For anyone using a screen reader or a keyboard, a good outline is often the fastest way to move through a fifty-page budget report or board packet, so building one is a real accessibility win, not just a nicety.

Build the outline so it mirrors the document's heading structure. Each top-level section should be a top-level bookmark, and subsections should nest underneath their parent, so the panel reads like a table of contents. Avoid a flat list of thirty same-level bookmarks, and avoid bookmarks that point to the wrong page or carry vague labels like "Section 2" — give each one the actual section title so it tells the reader where it goes.

In Acrobat Pro, open the Bookmarks panel and check that every major section is represented and correctly nested; you can drag bookmarks to re-parent them and right-click to rename. If you tagged headings well, you can generate a first draft of the outline from the document structure and then clean up the labels and nesting by hand. As a rule of thumb, any document long enough to need a table of contents is long enough to deserve bookmarks.

🔍 Reference: for the exact normative wording, see PDF/UA clause 7.17 in your ISO 14289-1 copy.
