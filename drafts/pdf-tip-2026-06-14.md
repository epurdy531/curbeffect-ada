Document Accessibility Tip of the Day

📃Topic: Put running headers and footers in Word's header and footer area, not in the body📄

When a page number, document title, or chapter name needs to repeat on every page, it belongs in Word's actual header or footer region — not typed into the top or bottom of the page as body text. The choice you make here in Word decides whether the exported PDF can correctly mark that repeating content as pagination, or whether it leaks into the reading flow and gets announced on every page by a screen reader.

In Word, open the Insert tab and use Header, Footer, or Page Number to place repeating content in the dedicated header and footer area. Use Word's built-in page number fields rather than typing numbers manually, and keep substantive content — body paragraphs, headings, captions — out of those regions entirely. If different sections need different headers (for example, front matter versus chapters), use section breaks and turn off Link to Previous so each section's header is set intentionally.

When you export with Save As PDF or Export with structure tags enabled, content placed in Word's header and footer area is carried into the PDF as pagination rather than as part of the main story. A remediator opening the file in Acrobat then has far less cleanup to do: page numbers and running titles can be marked as artifacts so assistive technology skips past them, instead of having to hunt down stray text frames sitting inside the body and re-tag them by hand.

🔍 Reference: for the exact normative wording, see PDF/UA clause 7.8 in your ISO 14289-1 copy.
