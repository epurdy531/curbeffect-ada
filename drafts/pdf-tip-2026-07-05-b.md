Document Accessibility Tip

📃Topic: Don't let color carry meaning on its own📄

Color is the accessibility problem that automated tag checkers sail right past — the tags can be perfect while the page is still unreadable — so it's worth handling by hand. Two habits cover most of it. First, never signal meaning with color alone: if "items in red are overdue," or a required form field is flagged only by a red label, add a word, symbol, or asterisk so someone who can't distinguish the color still gets the message. Second, check contrast — body text needs a **4.5:1** ratio against its background (**3:1** for large or bold headings), which rules out the light-gray-on-white and pale link colors that quietly creep in from templates. Choose colors with a contrast checker while you author in Word, and the exported PDF clears this bar with no rework later.

🔍 Reference: PDF/UA-1 leaves color and contrast to WCAG — see WCAG 1.4.1 (Use of Color) and 1.4.3 (Contrast (Minimum)).
