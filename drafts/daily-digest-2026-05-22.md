**A broad coalition of disability and civil-rights organizations — including the American Association of People with Disabilities and New York Lawyers for the Public Interest — has publicly condemned HHS's decision to push its Section 504 web and mobile accessibility deadline back a year, calling the delay unacceptable and warning that it will prolong barriers to federally funded healthcare for disabled people. The backlash underscores a point worth repeating to every covered organization: an extended compliance date moves the clock, not the underlying legal duty. Section 504's prohibition on disability-based discrimination has been in force since 1973, and private litigation can proceed regardless of the WCAG conformance date — the same logic that applies to the parallel ADA Title II extension for state and local governments. For HHS-funded hospitals, health systems, schools, and universities, the smartest use of the extra year is to keep remediating now rather than treat the new date as a reprieve. The growing advocacy pressure is also a clear signal that legal and political scrutiny of these entities is not going away.**
