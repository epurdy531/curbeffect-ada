Document Accessibility Tip

📃Topic: Combine automated and manual testing — checkers verify only part of accessibility📄

Automated accessibility checkers such as veraPDF or Acrobat's built-in checker are an essential first pass, but they only confirm the machine-testable slice of conformance — the structural rules a script can verify, like whether tags exist or the title is set. They cannot judge whether your alt text actually describes the image, whether heading levels reflect the document's real hierarchy, whether the reading order makes sense, or whether a table's header cells are logically associated with their data. Always follow the automated pass with a manual review: walk the tag tree, and listen to the document in a screen reader such as NVDA or VoiceOver, tabbing through links and form fields the way a real user would. The PDF Association's Matterhorn Protocol, which splits checkpoints into machine-checkable and human-verifiable, is a useful map of exactly what the tools miss.

🔍 Reference: automated tools cover only the machine-checkable checkpoints; for the human-verified ones, see the PDF Association's Matterhorn Protocol and PDF/UA clause 5 (Conformance) in your ISO 14289-1 copy.
