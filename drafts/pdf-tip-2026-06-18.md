Document Accessibility Tip of the Day

📃Topic: Use real multi-column layouts in Word, and keep meaningful content out of floating text boxes📄

When a Word document needs to look like a newsletter or a two-column report, it is tempting to fake the layout by dropping text boxes onto the page and dragging them into place. That choice almost always shows up later as a reading-order problem in the exported PDF, because floating boxes are not part of the document's main story flow and assistive technology has no reliable way to know which box to read first.

In Word, build columns with Layout, then Columns, and pick the number you actually need. Word lays the text down in a real continuous flow, so the export carries a single, predictable reading order from the top of column one to the bottom of the last column. If part of the page truly needs to stand apart, such as a pull quote or a sidebar, place it inline with the surrounding text and use a Word style for it, rather than anchoring a free-floating shape over the body.

Save text boxes and shapes for genuinely decorative ornament that can be marked as artifact in the PDF later. Anything a reader needs — headings, paragraphs, captions, callouts, instructions — belongs in the flowing body of the document so the export inherits a clean, linear reading order and you are not rebuilding it by hand in Acrobat.

🔍 Reference: for the exact normative wording, see PDF/UA clause 7.1 in your ISO 14289-1 copy.
