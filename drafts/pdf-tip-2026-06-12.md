Document Accessibility Tip of the Day

📃Topic: Run Word's Accessibility Checker before you export to PDF📄

Most accessibility problems in a tagged PDF are inherited straight from the Word document it came out of. The fastest way to catch them while they are still cheap to fix is to run Word's built-in Accessibility Checker on the source document, not on the PDF afterward.

In Word, open the Review tab and choose Check Accessibility. The pane on the right lists Errors, Warnings, and Tips, and clicking any item jumps you to the exact spot in the document. Common findings are missing alt text on pictures and charts, tables without a designated header row, low-contrast text, merged or split table cells, and content placed inside floating text boxes. Walk the list top to bottom and clear every Error and Warning before exporting.

The reason this matters for the PDF is that the checker's findings line up almost one-for-one with the structural problems a PDF/UA validator flags later: untagged images, tables without TH cells, reading-order surprises, and missing document language or title. Fixing them in Word means the export already has correct tags, alt text, table headers, and language attributes, and you spend remediation time on the few PDF-specific touches that remain instead of rebuilding structure cell by cell in Acrobat.

One habit that pays off: turn on "Keep accessibility checker running while I work" in the checker's options so issues surface as you write, and re-run a final pass right before you choose Save As PDF.

🔍 Reference: for the exact normative wording, see PDF/UA clause 7.1 in your ISO 14289-1 copy.
