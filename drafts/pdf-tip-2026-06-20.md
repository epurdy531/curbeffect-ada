Document Accessibility Tip of the Day

📃Topic: Exporting from Word — open the Options dialog and turn on accessibility tags📄

We've covered choosing Word's Save As / Export over "Print to PDF." Today, look one click deeper: the checkbox that actually preserves your structure lives inside the export dialog's Options button, and it's easy to miss.

When you choose File > Save As (or Export) and pick PDF, don't just hit Save. Click the Options button in that dialog first. In the box that opens, make sure "Document structure tags for accessibility" is checked, and check "Document properties" too so your Title and language travel into the PDF. Both are what carry your heading styles, lists, table headers, and alt text through to the exported file as real tags.

Watch the optimize setting next to the Save button. "Standard (publishing online and printing)" keeps tags reliably. "Minimum size" can strip or thin out structure to shrink the file, so avoid it for anything that needs to be accessible. If size is a concern, compress images in Word beforehand rather than trading away your tags on export.

One more habit: if you have the Acrobat add-in, the Acrobat ribbon's Create PDF / PDFMaker route honors these same settings and also turns your Heading styles into PDF bookmarks automatically — a nice bonus that saves outline work later. Whichever route you use, open the resulting PDF's tags afterward and confirm the structure came across; a single unchecked box is the most common reason a "tagged" export arrives flat.

🔍 Reference: for the exact normative wording, see PDF/UA clause 7.1 in your ISO 14289-1 copy.
