Document Accessibility Tip of the Day

📃Topic: Lists and list tagging📄

A list in a PDF only reads as a list to assistive technology when the tag structure says so. Visual bullets or numbers in the page content are not enough — without the right tags, screen readers announce one long paragraph instead of "list of five items" with item-by-item navigation.

In Acrobat Pro's Tags panel, every list should sit inside an <L> tag, with each item wrapped in an <LI>. Inside each <LI>, the bullet, number, or other marker belongs in an <Lbl> tag, and the item's actual content belongs in an <LBody>. That <L> > <LI> > <Lbl> + <LBody> shape is what gives screen readers the count, the marker, and the item text as separate, navigable pieces.

For nested lists, place the child <L> inside the parent <LI> (typically inside or alongside the <LBody>), not as a sibling of the outer list. That keeps the indentation level meaningful for assistive tech instead of flattening into one mixed sequence.

Set the ListNumbering attribute on the <L> tag to match what readers see — Disc, Circle, or Square for bulleted lists, Decimal, UpperRoman, LowerRoman, UpperAlpha, or LowerAlpha for ordered ones, and None when there is no marker. Mismatches between the visual marker and ListNumbering are a common remediation finding.

Finally, watch for "fake" lists made from hand-typed dashes, asterisks, or numbers followed by tabs — those usually come in tagged as plain <P> paragraphs and need to be retagged into a proper list structure rather than left as visual-only formatting.

🔍 Reference: for the exact normative wording, see PDF/UA clause 7.6 in your ISO 14289-1 copy.
