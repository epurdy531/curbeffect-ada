Document Accessibility Tip of the Day

📃Topic: Use Word's list buttons instead of typing dashes or numbers by hand📄

Lists look fine visually whether you type "- " in front of each item or click Word's bulleted-list button, but the two approaches produce very different PDFs. Only the real list buttons give the exported PDF actual list structure that a screen reader can recognize and navigate.

In Word, build every list with the Bulleted List or Numbered List buttons on the Home tab (or the Multilevel List button for nested items). Use Tab and Shift+Tab to demote and promote items rather than indenting with spaces, and let Word handle the numbering rather than typing "1.", "2.", "3." yourself — that way renumbering stays correct if items are inserted or reordered.

When you export to PDF with Save As or Export, those real lists carry through as proper <L>, <LI>, <Lbl>, and <LBody> tags. A screen reader can then announce "list of 5 items" up front, and the user can jump from item to item or skip the list entirely.

If you typed bullets by hand with dashes, asterisks, or manually numbered lines, the exported PDF will tag them as ordinary paragraphs. Remediating that later means rebuilding every list in the Tags panel cell by cell — slow work that the author could have prevented in seconds by clicking the right button up front.

A quick check before exporting: click into each list and confirm the corresponding list button on the ribbon is highlighted. If it isn't, the "list" is just styled paragraphs and needs to be reformatted with the real list control.

🔍 Reference: for the exact normative wording, see PDF/UA clause 7.6 in your ISO 14289-1 copy.
